Local Files Guide

The most reliable way to run walk-up songs on game day — iOS & Android

💡
Recommended for most teams. Local audio files play offline, don’t need a streaming subscription, and give WalkUp Songs full control over start time, duration, and fade in/out — which streaming sources often can’t match.

Why local files beat streaming at the field

📴
Works offline Ballfields often have spotty or no cell service. Once the MP3 is on your phone, playback doesn’t need Wi‑Fi or data.
💵
No streaming fee required You don’t need Apple Music, YouTube Premium, or Spotify Premium just to play walk-up songs. Free to set up and free to run on game day.
🎚️
Better fade & timing control Local files let WalkUp Songs start at the exact second you choose and fade in/out cleanly. Streaming APIs are often more limited or less predictable with fades.
✅
More reliable on game day No buffering, no account login surprises, no “song unavailable in your region,” and no second-device Spotify dance. Tap → play.
✂️
You can pre-trim the perfect 12–20 seconds Edit the clip at home so only the chorus/drop is in the file — then game day is dead simple.

What file types work?

Most teams use MP3. Also commonly supported: M4A, WAV, and AAC. Stick with MP3 if you’re unsure — it’s universal on both iPhone and Android.

Name files clearly before you import, e.g. 22-sandy-we-will-rock-you.mp3, so they’re easy to find in the picker.

Where to get the audio files

Prefer sources you’re allowed to use. Walk-up music at youth games is short and local, but copyright still applies — especially if you post recordings online.

Recommended sources

🛒
Buy or own the track Purchase from iTunes, Amazon, Bandcamp, and similar stores — or rip a CD you already own.
👨‍👩‍👧
Ask families for a short clip Parents often already have their kid’s favorite song as an MP3 and can text or email it over.
🎵
Royalty-free or custom audio A solid choice for teams that want zero copyright stress. Pair with the app’s AI announcer for a full intro.

YouTube → MP3 (common, but be careful)

⚠️
Copyright & safety note. Downloading audio from YouTube can violate YouTube’s terms and may infringe copyright depending on the track and how you use it. Random “free MP3 converter” websites are also a common source of adware and malware. Use legal sources when you can, and only convert content you’re allowed to use.

If you still choose to convert a YouTube video to audio for personal/team use:

Prefer a desktop tool

A local converter on a computer is safer than random websites. Search for reputable open-source options like yt-dlp instead of shady online converters.

Be careful with extensions & sites

“YouTube to MP3” extensions and converters vary wildly. Skip anything that pushes weird permissions, pop-up downloads, or “enable notifications” traps.

Export a short clip

Keep only the walk-up section — usually 12–20 seconds. Smaller files, clearer starts, fewer surprises on game day.

Listen before you import

Open the MP3 on your computer and confirm it’s the right moment before loading the whole lineup onto the phone.

Pro tip: After converting, trim the file in a free editor (Audacity, CapCut, or a phone audio trimmer) so the file starts on the chorus. Then you barely need start-offset fiddling in the app.

Get the files onto your phone

iPhone / iPad

  • AirDrop or Files app — send the MP3 from a Mac/PC, or save it into the Files app (iCloud Drive or On My iPhone).
  • Email / Messages — send the file to yourself, open the attachment, tap Share → Save to Files.
  • Google Drive / Dropbox — download the file to Files / Downloads so WalkUp Songs can pick it.
  • Computer sync (optional) — add songs to the Music app via Finder/iTunes, then choose them from Music Library in WalkUp Songs.

Android

  • Downloads folder — download the MP3 in Chrome; it usually lands in Downloads.
  • USB from a computer — plug in the phone, copy MP3s into Music or Download.
  • Drive / email / messaging — open the attachment and tap Download / Save.
  • Keep them in one folder — e.g. a “WalkUp Songs” folder makes picking files much faster on game week.

How to add a local file in WalkUp Songs

  • Open your team and tap the player.
  • Choose the option to set / change their walk-up song.
  • Select Local file, Files, or Music Library (wording can vary slightly by platform).
  • Pick the MP3 from Files / Downloads / your music library.
  • Set the start time, duration, and fade out so the best 12–20 seconds play every at-bat.
  • Tap play once at home (or in the parking lot) to confirm volume and timing before first pitch.
Android tip: If the file picker doesn’t show your song, confirm it’s an MP3/M4A in Downloads or Music, then try the system file browser. Grant storage / media permission if Android asks.
iOS tip: If you imported via the Files app, browse On My iPhone or iCloud Drive inside the picker. If you synced through the Music app, use Music Library instead.

Game-day tips that make local files shine

🔋
Do the setup at home Import every player’s song the night before. Game day should only be Bluetooth + Play Ball.
🔊
Connect the speaker early Pair Bluetooth before warmups. Local playback is rock-solid once the phone and speaker are talking.
🗂️
Keep a backup set of files Store the team’s MP3 folder in Google Drive or iCloud. If a phone dies mid-season, you’re back online in minutes.
⬇️
Mark files “Keep Downloaded” On iPhone, open the Files app, long-press each song (or the whole folder), and choose Keep Downloaded so iCloud doesn’t offload them to the cloud. Without that, a song can look available at home and then fail at the field with no signal. On Android, download files to device storage (Downloads / Music) instead of leaving them cloud-only.
📵
Turn on airplane mode + Bluetooth (optional) Prevents surprise calls/notifications from interrupting playback while still allowing your speaker connection.
⌚
Pair with Apple Watch if you have one Local songs still play from the phone/speaker — the watch is just a convenient trigger from the dugout.
